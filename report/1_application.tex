\subsection{Introduction to the service and problem domain}
Collective housing, in danish known as "Kollektiv", is a variant of co-living, where individuals share the same living space, such as an apartment or a house. 
In addition to the shared space, residents typically also share other fixed monthly expenses such as electricity and heat.
As prices and the pressure to live more sustainably rises, collective housing stands out as a cost-effective and ressource-efficient alternative
that also creates opportunities for social interaction, mutual support and connection with like-minded individuals.
Today, however, the process of finding a collective is often complicated, usually requiring you to rely on your personal
network of family and friends, along with browsing social media groups and posts.

Our web service, ''Kollektivportalen.dk'' aims to solve this problem by providing a portal for matching seekers with providers of collectives.
The goal of our service is to provide an intuitive interface for both parties, that allows seekers to easily discover and 
find collectives that suit their preferences and providers to easily advertise their collectives and find suitable residents.
By gathering all relevant information in one place, our service provides a more structured and organized process of matching seekers and collectives.
The implementation has been carried out in the spirit of considering how we realistically would solve such a problem and acts as a prototype for our Innovation Project.


The overall design revolves around the deliberate choice of two distinct user groups: seekers of collectives and providers of collectives.
Users choose whether they are a seeker or provider when registering.
Through role-based access control these user groups are granted distinct possibilities on the site.
From a business perspective, however, an essential feature is that all users, even anonymous ones, can browse the collectives on the site.
This allows potential users to quickly gain an overview of possible collectives, 
thereby encouraging seekers to create an account and motivating potential providers to list their own living solutions. 
This rationale justifies several design decisions throughout our implementation, such as the inclusion of a search bar on the landing page, and the 
ability to apply filters when searching for collectives.


Our current implementation has a landing page, an overview of all collectives via a persistent database with the ability to filter by city, price and size,
an overview of owned collectives for providers aswell as the ability to create new collectives, and a profile page for the seekers where personal information can be updated.

One important feature that has been planned but not yet implemented is support for applications.
For seekers, this would include the ability to apply to collectives and see an overview of sent applications and for providers
to receive and review incoming applications.
This service would aim to streamline the process of applying to collectives by centralizing application submission
and providing relevant applicant information through the seeker's profile data.
The skeleton for this future feature can already be seen on the site with the ''Apply'' button on each collective.

\subsection{High-level overview}

The visual identity of the service is build using the Bootstrap framework. 
This choice enables quicker deployment due to the ease of styling via the inherent CSS. 
However, we have tried to stay mindful of good accessibility and semantics of the HTML. 
In the following, we invite the reader on a guided tour of the website, highlighting the core
design choices and functionality.

\subsubsection{Landing page}

The tour begins at the landing page which serves as the primary entry point to the service.
Users are welcomed by a prominent hero section featuring a header, a search bar and a large scale image.
Below this, a view of a few selected collectives and a small introduction to our service can be found. 

We have tried to deliberately make our landing page visually pleasing and have it provide a nice user experience by
clearly stating the main goal of the service within the opening hero section.
Users are immediately encouraged to explore the database by searching for collectives in a city of their liking. 
The few selected collectives on the landing page serve as advertisements for the platform's offerings. 
In the future exactly which collectives are featured could be dynamically selected based on a recommendation system or as promotion for specific collectives.

The landing page remains accessible to both registered and unregistered users, and is always 
accessible via the top left corner of the navigation bar. 
The layout of the page scales nicely with differently formatted viewports; e.g with mobile phones. 

\subsubsection{Page navigation}

Users can easily navigate the site through the navigation bar displayed at the top of every page.
The content of the navigation bar depends on the authentication of the current user. 
The bar automatically adjusts to the size of the viewport, collapsing into a ''hamburger'' style menu when necessary. 
Authentication controls are located on the right, providing options to login or register, and, once logged in,
displaying the user's status as ''Logged in'' along with a log-out option. 
The left hand side features the core functionality of the service, with the global access to the view of collectives, 
and navigation to profile management for seekers and collective management for providers.

\subsubsection{Overview of collectives}

Perhaps the most important part of our service is the explorative element of collectives in our database. 
This is provided with a card-based album overview, that displays an image and information about the size in squaremeters, the monthly expenses, 
and a button to apply straight away. 
Each card is also clickable, leading to a detailed page with more elaborative information. 
All users can apply filters to discover specific collectives within their preferences. 

\subsubsection{Seeker specific functionality}

Seeker specific functionality encompass the ability to update your profile with personal information via a form. 
This includes persistent storage of a profile picture, name, birthdate, gender, occupation and a short biography. This is implemented as a lazy profile creation, as the profile can be created on demand after being registered as a user. 
This information is intended to be visible to providers when reviewing applications to ease finding the right
fit for the collective. When application functionality is implemented in the future, applying to collectives will only be possible for seekers.
Here the profile page could be extended to also allow for viewing the status of your applications.

\subsubsection{Provider specific functionality}

Only users with the provider role can access the site for creating new collectives.
This page consists of a form where the provider must specify the city, streetname, monthly expenses, square meters, a longer description and a picture.
When the collective has been submitted, it becomes part of the global overview of all collectives, and can instantly be seen by potential seekers.
It is also visible on the provider-specific page "Your Collectives".
This functions as the provider's profile page but instead of having personal information they see an overview of their collectives on the site.
In a future iteration, the provider would also be able to see and review applications for their collective. 
Ideally, we want to create an intuitive overview of applicants; where they can access the application, 
but also clearly see the profile data of the seeker. This is an important part of our problem domain, 
as each collective traditionally has their own distinctive procedure for deciding upon new members. 
Each collective is free to decide on this procedure outside of our service, but
we would aspire to ease the process by centralizing the receival of applications and providing details through the seeker's profile data.
